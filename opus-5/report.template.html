Benchmark summary report

Five query engine configurations across TPC-H and TPC-DS

A consolidated view of decision-support benchmark timings for Spark Rust 0.42.1 (default and tuned), DuckDB 1.5.5, Spark 4.1.1 with Gluten and Spark 4.2 — measured single-node at four scale factors, and on an eight-worker cluster at three scale factors with CPU and memory accounting. Every figure on this page is rendered directly from the accompanying summary dataset; nothing is estimated or filled in.

01 Headline results

Aggregate totals are the sum of per-query wall-clock seconds over all queries at all scale factors in the dataset. Ratios compare each engine against DuckDB 1.5.5, the dataset's reference baseline.

Read the two tiers separately. The single-node and cluster runs use different hardware, different engine builds and different query sets; the dataset provides no cross-tier normalisation, so single-node totals are not comparable with cluster totals.

          06 Reading notes and limitations

          What this dataset does and does not establish.

          Fields and units

          • time — wall-clock seconds. Suite rows are the sum over their queries.
          • cpu — CPU-seconds consumed; cores is exactly cpu ÷ time, i.e. mean concurrent cores, not a core allocation.
          • memtime — GB-seconds of resident memory; mean is memtime ÷ time. Both relationships were checked against every row.
          • p50 / p95 / max — memory distribution in GB over the run, not latency percentiles.

          Undefined in the source data

          • The per-query markers B and P appear on six single-node rows with no legend in the dataset; they are reproduced verbatim and left uninterpreted.
          • canonical and matched are carried through as provided. Where they are null the source supplied no value, which is shown as n/a rather than zero.
          • Repeat counts, cold/warm cache state, storage layout and query-validation procedure are not part of this dataset.

          Coverage gaps

            Interpretation cautions

            • Summed suite totals are dominated by the slowest queries; the per-query tables show where a difference actually comes from.
            • Single-node and cluster tiers ran on different hardware and are not directly comparable.
            • These are not audited or publishable TPC results — no TPC-H or TPC-DS compliance claim is made or implied.
